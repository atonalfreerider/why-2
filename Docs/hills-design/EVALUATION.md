# The economy, seen for the first time

An evaluation of how understandable the why-2 economy visualization is, from the starting view through all 22 economy views, the replay, the universe scene's civilization views and both guided tours. It merges twelve reviews, each checked finding by finding by a skeptic, and settles their conflicts against the code at commit 8930476 (3 November 2026). A prioritized backlog of 51 items accompanies it. Item ids (Q01-Q27 quick fixes, S01-S21 structural fixes, M1-M3 new modalities) are cited throughout.

**Corrections (3 Nov 2026 design pass).** The design pass for the new views ([MODALITIES.md](MODALITIES.md), section 2) recomputed these figures from the repository's data. The direction between tiers and 5.1's fourth acceptance test are corrected in place (the roots row in section 2, 5.1, S11 and M1); the totals are for any caption or narration that cites them.

- **Direction between tiers.** In the stored 2024 use table (`circuit.json` io: rows are suppliers, columns are buyers, money runs from buyer to supplier, and own-industry purchases are left out), industries paid other industries $15.85T. Of that, 37% went up to suppliers in a higher tier, 22% went down and 41% stayed within a tier, counting the state as the bottom tier ($2.09T of the "up" is the state buying supplies; with its other purchases from industries, $1.07T in `circuit.json` finalDemand, it buys $3.16T from them, as MODALITIES.md's rule 4 counts it). Among market tiers alone, 28% went up, 25% down and 47% stayed within. Supplies come down; the money goes up. This report first stated the direction backwards.
- **Where tech stands out.** In 2025 tech was 9.4% of private value added and about 9.6% of gross operating surplus, what is left after wages and production taxes. No 2025 surplus is stored: the 9.6% is 2025 value added at each industry's 2024 split (`industries.json` valueAdded × gosShare2024), and the 2024 accounts in `circuit.json` io give 9.45%. Tech was 16.5% of domestic corporate profits (BEA Table 6.16D in `instant.json`, with BEA's industry lines mapped to the tiers by assumption). That share is larger because the surplus also covers the wear of buildings and machines, interest, rent and the earnings of the self-employed, which weigh less in tech: profits are 51% of its estimated surplus and 27% of the other tiers'. It holds at least 31.7% of US listed value: its 10 stored firms are $23.9T of $75.3T (`circuit.json` capture). Its weight shows most in market value, so 5.1's acceptance now asks for "a larger share of market value than of output", not of profit.
- **Measured totals.** Captions draw measured totals; the simulation supplies splits only, marked ≈:
  - household spending is BEA's PCE, $20.95T in 2025 (`circuit.json` personal.pce; $20.87T after BEA's September 2026 annual update), not the simulation's $21.63T (Q04);
  - household debt is the DFA's $20.0T at 2026:Q1, every household liability, of which home mortgages and consumer credit are $18.9T (`instant.json`), not the simulation's $14.35T (3.1, S16);
  - $185.8T of household net worth is the DFA's 2026:Q2 headline (`circuit.json` groupNotes; the six income groups in `instant.json` sum to $185.7T for that quarter); the 2026:Q1 groups the scene uses sum to $174.0T;
  - "the US buys $1.2T more than it sells" is the current account ($1.19T in 2024 and $1.17T in 2025, NIPA Table 4.1), not the trade gap in goods and services ($0.88T in 2024 and $0.92T in 2025, NIPA Table 1.1.5; both tables in `instant.json`);
  - the 13 economies of the world view hold 55% of the world's people (2024, `global.json`), not 45%.

## 1. Summary

### What a first-time viewer understands today

The universe scene leaves the viewer with one memorable chain: matter (red), life (green) and people (blue), from the Big Bang to Now. Its civilizations section teaches the wrong lesson. History is a thin ribbon that "explodes" after 1800 into tufts labelled with footballers and pop stars. With "Relative power" in the subtitle and "up: hierarchy" in the legend, viewers read a power ranking with Lionel Messi's region on top and the United States at the bottom. The rise is actually the west-to-east axis seen from a 60° camera. No civilization, state or form of government can be read by name.

The economy opens with a hard cut to a glowing gold mountain range on a grey slab labelled GOVERNMENT, under a cloud of white stars. In plain words, a viewer leaves with:

- The economy is big, layered and historical, and gold means money.
- Government is underneath everything.
- Money climbs from every hill to owners at the top. Tech is the highest hill, so it must be worth the most (the legend says "height = market value").
- Famous people float above it all.
- The few legible flat charts and panels add three more things:
  - Money was dearest in 1981 and nearly free in 2009-2016, and tariffs tripled in 2025.
  - People move between income fifths over a decade, and falls come with lost jobs and divorce.
  - "I am at the 58th percentile, and most of the income my spending pays goes to people above me."

Viewers do not leave with any of the following:

- The tiers standing on one another.
- AI and social-media companies at the summit, pushing a frontier.
- The lower tiers both supporting the top and being dominated by it.
- Every dollar spent toward a desire or away from a fear. This is the scene's own "fundamental structure", and only the mind view shows it.
- How a dollar splits. They will think owners keep nearly all of it; the model gives wages 41¢ and owners 27¢.
- Class as something comparable across industries.
- The United States as one economy among others that trade and fight.

They also read time backwards. The 3D views run the past to the right and away from the viewer, the opposite of the universe scene and of the economy's own charts, and the only arrow on screen, "FUTURE →", points at the past.

### The five biggest obstacles

1. **The picture contradicts its own words.** Some views exist to show desire and fear, the dollar's split, the classes, cooperation, or one person's mind. In those views the subject is the faintest thing on screen:
   - In the rivers view, rose and ice make up a few percent of the coloured pixels; gold makes up roughly 80-90%.
   - In capture, wages (41¢) are drawn at 30% strength and owners (27¢) at full, pulsing HDR.
   - The society web is one lavender mass, and the betrayal view is the same image.
   - The mind is buried under company labels 40-60 px tall.

   Three root causes: every hill layer runs its own emphasis switch; bloom is set to 1.9 (1.15 in the universe), so overlaps turn white; and gold halos, the white crest and the star cloud stay at full strength in almost every view.
2. **The keys are wrong, and they present assumptions as measurements.**
   - The only legend on screen is left over from the retired "bowl" design. It is also the only key during the tour. It reads "Land: one year · area = value · height = market value · dots: 100,000 people each", and its "Fantasy" swatch matches nothing drawn.
   - Each hue carries 5-9 meanings across views, and the blue for people and the ice for fear are the same hue in practice.
   - Each view's own key is a 13 px grey subtitle drawn over the scene, hidden during the tour.
   - The year readout prints the author's fixed category weights ("fear 54%") as if they sat alongside GDP as statistics.
3. **Height, width and time change meaning from view to view.**
   - "Class is altitude" actually draws the industry's assumed tier height. Everyone below about the 55th income percentile stands at one height, and public employees sit with the out of work.
   - Height inside the status cloud is a graph-layout coordinate. Company height is log market value riding a ridge that itself grows with output.
   - Undisclosed scales flatten exactly the inequalities on show: 11 times the real output draws as about 3.3 times the width, and a $3T firm stands 2.3 times as tall as a $10B one.
   - Time runs back and to the right in the 3D wave, but left to right everywhere else.
4. **Nothing can be found or followed.**
   - The bar of 22 views renders at 5-6 px, and 12 views have no key.
   - 16 of 20 tour stops name marks they never point at; 6 stops are silent and 4 play outdated audio.
   - 3D labels are 3-8 px from the starting distance and 40-60 px in close views, with no collision handling.
   - The tour shows effects before their causes and ends on the opening frame without assembling the picture.
5. **Most of the author's structure has no picture yet.**
   - There is no instantaneous, balance-sheet view of classes and corporate tiers.
   - There is no global economy: no other economies, bilateral trade, or war and conflict.
   - Nothing marks the symbiosis between the tech summit and the tiers below, or the frontier.
   - The storylines are one extreme climber at a time: no one who falls, no one who stays, and no one's visibility changing over time.

### Overall grade

**D+, about 2 out of 5.** The 22 economy views average 1.9, and none scores above 3. The underlying work is strong and would support a B:
- measured accounts with sources;
- a calibrated population model;
- Jev judgments kept apart in the documentation;
- a vertical metaphor (government, value chain, tech summit, capital, attention) that people remember.

What fails:
- the rendering (emphasis, bloom, colour, labels);
- the keys;
- navigation and the tour;
- three things the author asked for and that do not exist yet: an instantaneous structure view, the world economy, and storylines that rise and fall in value and visibility.

### Where to start

The first seven backlog items are small edits that stop the picture contradicting itself:
- a true legend (Q01);
- bloom as a highlight rather than the default (Q02);
- the rivers and capture views at true weight (Q03, Q04);
- person views cleared of company labels (Q05);
- a camera bug that leaves strategy in a stale 1972 pose (Q06);
- a screenshot harness that captures the right stops (Q07).

Next come the readability fixes (Q08-Q24). Then build four shared foundations: one emphasis table, one palette, a per-view legend and a screen-space label system (S01-S04). Only then build the three new modalities, so they do not inherit today's defects (M1, M2, M3). Data collection for the world economy can start immediately.

## 3. Cross-cutting problems

### 3.1 Visual language and colour

**One legend, six hues, about thirty meanings.** The legend promises Capital gold, People blue, Desire rose, Fear ice, State steel and Fantasy white. Twelve of the fourteen hill layers declare their own colour constants; only the wave and replay layers use the shared EconomyStyle. What each hue ends up meaning:

| Hue | Legend | Also means, somewhere in the scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gold | Capital | owners' surplus and profit, saving, investment, the federal funds rate, "need" (company lines), "climbed", "in control", exports, private summits, in-tribe cooperation, rate events |
| Rose | Desire | "fell", the 30-year mortgage, advertising, recessions, debt rings, net payers, defection, the other tribe, private spending lanes |
| Ice | Fear | the 10-year Treasury, imports, the Social Security and Medicare pillars, the GOVERNMENT and WAKE labels, job loss, Democrats |
| Blue | People | wages, labour, cooperation, input-output roots; renders white where dense |
| White | Fantasy (no hills mark draws it) | the crest ("now"), the attention cloud, votes, direction arrows and deals, the YOU and person figures, the parents' ghost, mobility rung bars, the decorative spray, and anything that overlaps under bloom |
| Steel | State | eight different steels; "stayed"; the tariff series |
| Red, green | no swatch | raw matter and life (carried over from the universe scene), bankruptcies, defection |

**People and fear are one colour.** The layers draw people and wages at about 206-208° and fear at 200-204°. The legend's own People (223°) and Fear (187°) are used by no layer. Strategy asks the viewer to tell "blue: its workers" from "ice: fear", and the you view asks them to tell fear rivers from wages. Neither is possible.

**Motives are blended, not split.** A person's or a company's motive mix is drawn as a blend: through warm white in one layer, through violet in another. Most households sit at 51-58% fear, so most marks land on white or violet, colours the legend does not have. Only the split trunks of the rivers keep rose and ice apart, and those are nearly invisible.

**What should hold** (where the reviewers converge):
- one palette in EconomyStyle, with no layer-local colours;
- rose and ice only for motive, always shown as a split;
- gold for owners' claims and profit; blue for people and wages; slate for every government mark;
- white only for attention, as in the universe scene's popular lines, with the crest a neutral tagged "the year shown";
- red and green swatches for raw matter and life;
- direction and sign (climbed or fell, in or out, cooperate or defect) carried by arrowheads, dashes and position, not by hue;
- self-contained charts (money, mobility, regime strata) with their own key, hues outside the semantic set, and labels at the line ends (S02, S03).

**What height, width and time mean.**

- **Height encodes at least six things:**
  - on the hills, the assumed order of tiers plus ridge relief from the owners' share and the square root of value added;
  - for people, the you view and the ladder, income rank within the hill that pays you;
  - for company lines and towers, log market value above a ridge;
  - inside the attention cloud, a graph-layout coordinate;
  - on the money chart, percent a year;
  - on the mobility ladder, income fifth.

  The legend says "height = market value" for all of them.
- **Class altitude is the most consequential.** The three lowest rungs lie beyond the hill's foot, where its height is zero, so the out of work, the working poor and frontline workers stand at one height, and everyone below about the 55th percentile is flat. Absolute height is mostly the industry's tier: office workers on a tech hill stand 3-4 times higher than owners at the top of the oil and gas hill. Government-paid people (teachers, soldiers, retirees) sit on the valley floor labelled "out of work".
- **Undisclosed compressive scales** flatten the inequalities the author wants to show:
  - the wave widens with the square root of real output, so 11 times the output since 1947 draws as about 3.3 times the width;
  - a $3T company stands 2.3 times as tall as a $10B one (log), and its beam carries linear "$1T" ticks inside a log length;
  - wealth rings and star sizes are log;
  - attention-loop widths are log, and two of its legs are sized by invented fractions of household spending.
- **Width** follows at least four rules in one scene: linear value-added bands; the square root for the wave and most flows, with coefficients from .045 to .9; log for company lines and rings; constant pixels. A stock (household debt, $14.35T) is drawn beside a flow (saving, $1.08T a year) with a width factor six times smaller.
- **Time** runs back and to the right in the 3D wave views; in the companies view the road reads 2026 on the left and 1935 on the right. It runs left to right in the universe scene, the money chart, the mobility ladder and Docs/ECONOMY.md ("from 1946 (left) to now (right)"). The "FUTURE →" label always faces the camera, so its arrow always points screen-right, into the past.

  The cause is geometric. The hills rise to the right of the road as one faces the future, so any pose that keeps the valley in front puts the future on the left. Turning the camera alone (yaw 140° to 220°) moves it to the summit side and stands the tall tech hills in front of the lower tiers. Keeping the composition with the future on the right needs a mirror reflection of the cross-slope axis and new poses for every view. The alternative is to state that "the past recedes behind the crest" and move the companies' lives onto a flat left-to-right chart. This is the author's decision (S07); the arrow can be fixed now (Q17).

### 3.4 Clutter and bloom

- **Bloom is the default state, not a highlight.** The hills use bloom 1.9 with scatter .80 over a 0.9 threshold; the universe scene uses 1.15 and .72. Most data lines are additive at HDR intensity 2-8, so overlaps add up to white: 52-62% of the bright pixels in the overview, rivers, people and social frames are near-white. Colour is lost exactly where the data is densest: the 75 company lines at the crest, the cloud's core, the person's ladder path. White, the legend's "Fantasy", is produced by overlap rather than drawn.
- **Every view looks the same.** EconomyViews.Table, the per-view settings table, states the right rule: the view's story at full strength, everything else at 0.02-0.15. In the hills only the road reads it; each layer runs its own emphasis switch. The crest (HDR 3.2), the gold halos that "breathe so the eye finds the gold first", and the star cloud stay at full strength in nearly every view. Landscape, people, rivers and roots differ from each other by 31-34 out of 255 on average, and society and betrayal by about 2.
- **Off-topic layers switch on.**
  - Jev's regulatory-capture strands appear at full strength in the value-capture view; the shared word "capture" switched them on.
  - The attention loop's arc leaks into society as its only red stroke.
  - Firm org charts and readouts flood the person views.
  - Owners' gold threads rise through the companies view.
- **Tangles instead of bundles.**
  - In capture, up to 10 gold arcs per halo across 23 halos, plus inner-circle threads, converge off the top of the frame.
  - About 1,000 society ties form one band.
  - Hundreds of strands hang in front of the crest face in the landscape view.
- **The fix is structural:**
  - one per-view emphasis table that every hill layer reads (S01);
  - bloom kept for hover and selection (Q02);
  - normal alpha blending, not additive glow, for marks that encode amounts;
  - bundled flows whose widths add up (S16).

## 5. What new display modalities must do

These are requirements, not designs. The four views that communicate best today (money, mobility, mind and the regimes panel) share three traits:
- each steps out of the wave onto a plane of its own;
- each labels its marks directly;
- each tells one story.

The new modalities should keep those habits and use the third dimension for layering and context, not to encode quantities that must be compared.

### 5.0 Requirements for all three

1. One story per view: the subject at full strength, everything else at 15% or less, or absent. No bloom on colour that carries meaning.
2. A key generated from the view's own encodings, visible during the tour and in the headset. No hue reused for another meaning.
3. Every number marked as measured (with source and year), judged by Jev (with confidence), or assumed or simulated. Synthetic people named as synthetic.
4. Legible: text at least 12 px on a 1080p desktop and about 1° of visual angle in the headset; labels never overlap; nothing important under a panel.
5. Units and scales stated on screen: dollars a year versus stocks, people per mark. Linear or area-true by default; any log or square-root scale printed.
6. Where time appears, the past is on the left and the future on the right, as in the universe scene and Docs/ECONOMY.md.
7. Reachable by a key, from the bar, from the tour and from the headset menu, with one tour stop each.
8. A first-time viewer can answer the view's questions (the acceptance tests below) without the narration.

### 5.1 The instantaneous structure view (balance sheet and income statement)

Purpose: show, at one instant, how the classes and the corporate tiers depend on and build on one another, with no time dimension.

1. **One year, no time axis.** No wake, ribs, road or lifelines on screen (absent, not faint). The frame states the year.
2. **Two states of one frame**, switched by the viewer:
   - **Balance sheet (stocks):** assets (home, equity and funds, deposits) and debts by class; value added and market value by tier; the state's debt.
   - **Income statement (the year's flows):** household spending from each class to each tier, split into desire and fear; wages from tiers to classes; owners' surplus to the owning classes and abroad; purchases between tiers in both directions; taxes to the state; transfers and public services back; imports and exports at the edge.
3. **Classes on one national scale** (income or wealth percentile: five fifths and the top 1%), never as altitude relative to an industry's hill. Each class shows its make-up by role (owners, employees, public payroll, not working, retired) with head counts.
4. **Tiers stacked bottom to top** in the author's order (government, raw materials, manufacturing and infrastructure, services, tech). Width is linear in value added. Tech's market value and profit share stand beside its value-added share, so dominance is measured, not asserted.
5. **Interdependence in both directions, with the measured totals:** of the $15.85T industries paid other industries in 2024, 37% went up to suppliers in a higher tier, 22% down and 41% stayed within a tier, counting the state as the bottom tier (28%, 25% and 47% among market tiers alone). Supplies come down; the money goes up. Tech buys $0.89T from the tiers below and sells them $0.80T, plus $0.29T to the state. "Supports" and "dominates" appear only as judged overlays, marked as such.
6. **One dollar scale for every flow, with a printed scale bar.** Stocks are drawn as pools, never on the flow scale. The household dollar's split sums to 100% and says "before income taxes" while income taxes are left out.
7. **The state as buyer, employer and payer**, not only as a tax sink.
8. **Capital and attention kept apart:** the top 1% (capital) and the attention cloud are separate and labelled.
9. **Entry points:** from the overview ("stop time"), by a key, and as the tour's synthesis stop.

Acceptance: within 30 seconds, without narration, a first-time viewer can say:
- which tier produces the most and which is valued the most;
- how a household dollar splits;
- which classes own the capital and which earn wages;
- that the top tier buys from the tiers beneath it and holds a larger share of market value than of output;
- where the state takes money in and pays it out.

Data: all in the repository except market capitalization by sector. Start with the 25 stored firms and flag the gap.

### 5.2 The global economy

Purpose: show the United States as one economy within a world economy, with competition, trade and conflict.

1. **Several economies on the same time axis.** The US alongside the largest economies (China, the EU, Japan, India, the UK, Russia and the Soviet Union, Saudi Arabia) and its largest trade partners (Canada, Mexico), plus an explicit rest of the world, so shares sum to 100%.
2. **Each economy sized by its measured share of world output**, ordered across the time axis from west to east, as in the universe scene's civilization streams, with the ordering stated on screen. This continues the universe scene's "Power shifts again" streams, so the hand-off between scenes is continuous.
3. **Each economy on its own foundation** (form of government; state spending and revenue as shares of GDP; military spending) and with its own resources (energy and mineral rents, arable land). Today's "the same US hills on another state" may remain only as a labelled thought experiment.
4. **Trade bilateral and in both directions**, so balances show (the US-China deficit), with partners named; total world trade and the US share of it.
5. **Conflict as dated events, distinct in form from trade:** tariff rounds and retaliation (2018-2019, 2025), embargoes (1980 grain), sanctions (Russia 2022), export controls (chips), wars. Each drawn so its effect on trade is visible. No hue shared with raw-matter red or desire rose.
6. **Dependence as layers the viewer turns on**, never all drawn at once: who holds US Treasuries; chokepoints in chips, rare earths and oil.
7. **One world economy:** a visible world total and each economy's share, plus a one-year trade matrix as the balance-sheet reading. Optionally, attention across borders: the most-followed accounts worldwide and their pull on American attention.
8. **Missing data shown as missing**, never filled in silently. Every series sourced and dated.

Acceptance: a viewer can say:
- which economies are largest and how that changed since 1950;
- who trades most with whom, and in which direction;
- whether the US buys more than it sells, and from whom;
- when conflict cut trade;
- what share of the world the US is.

Data to acquire:

| Data | Possible sources |
| --- | --- |
| Output by country | IMF, World Bank, Maddison |
| Bilateral trade | UN Comtrade or IMF Direction of Trade Statistics |
| Military spending | SIPRI |
| Foreign holders of Treasuries | Treasury International Capital (TIC) |
| Resource rents | World Bank |

### 5.3 Individual storylines

Purpose: let viewers follow people who stay, rise or fall in economic value and in visibility.

1. **Several lives at once.** At least one person who climbs, one who stays and one who falls, chosen as typical members of the same starting group rather than extremes. The viewer can add their own projected path.
2. **Economic value on one national scale** (income percentile and net worth), time left to right, parents' rank as a dashed reference. Never position relative to an industry's hill.
3. **Life events named at their years** (first job, move, marriage, home, child, job loss, divorce, inheritance, retirement), each with what changed.
4. **Visibility as a second track on the same time axis, only where dated data exist** (follower or page-view series for real public figures). Synthetic people show "visibility not modelled". A 2025 snapshot is never drawn as history.
5. **At least one life where money and visibility move together** (a capital holder who is also highly followed), and one where they part.
6. **Each life against its base rate:** how many people like them climbed, stayed or fell, with n and a range. Events are shown as associations from the simulation, not as causes.
7. **Real and synthetic people labelled.** Synthetic people appear as "synthetic person #N".
8. **Works where the people live** (on the hill, in the cloud) and as a 2D strip readable in the headset, with a chooser that works by hand.

Acceptance: a viewer can follow three lives and say:
- what happened to each, and when;
- how rare each path is;
- whether visibility followed money.

## 7. Method

**Groups.** Twelve review groups each produced a scored evaluation and passed it to a skeptic.

Eight covered sets of views:
- the temporal starting views and the replay;
- the civilization views and the bridge into the economy;
- money flows and the state (capture, rivers, foundation, roots, money);
- people, classes and storylines (people, mobility, you, ladder, atom, dilemma, mind);
