# Economy landscape implementation - 2026-10-02

The latest user review supersedes SPEC2's benches and literal crowns. One continuous holographic mountain range rises through government, raw materials, manufacturing/infrastructure, services and tech. It occupies roughly 190 by 190 world units in XZ with over 70 units of relief; no floating platforms or opaque terrain skins are rendered. This is the requested conceptual production lens, not an empirical ranking of economic importance. Industry mountains interlock on a shared rising terrain field, with inhabited valleys and slopes. The existing Why/Line shader and shared HDR bloom produce the elevation contours and summit rims. Diversified wealth floats above the stack.

## Geometry and ownership

Industry footprint area follows stored value added and sums to 18000 square world units. Local relief reflects operating-surplus share; decorative ridges are not measured authority. Stable positions permit historical comparison. Gold bands encircle the hilltops. Interior blue employee glyphs branch through management toward gold capital claims. This branching and the supporting strands between strata are schematic, not verified organization charts, legal control relationships or input-output measurements. Company markers use the existing company snapshot. Two named voting-control examples use dated SEC filings; all other rings are a general ownership metaphor. Employment does not imply voting rights.

Wage groups inhabit their industry's lowlands, business owners its slopes, and diversified top-wealth groups cloud pools. Child satellites and selected support/inheritance paths reuse the population and historical lives records. Population relationships include partner links and two-parent birth junctions in the mid-plane, scoped to Economy. Original population samples are unchanged.

Investment climbs hills; wage, spending and measured input-output routes have separate views. Routes illustrate flows, not hydraulic volumes. Household spending follows the existing category crosswalk. Small routes are omitted for legibility.

## Interactive program

The detached 3D mind view displays the selected group's memory, desire, fear, deliberation, spending choice and feedback. Inspect/simulate controls select a group, adjust fear/reason, step or play annual scenarios, and reset. Historical balances remain immutable.

The counterfactual fixes starting composition and income/tax flows. It does not forecast demography, market returns, inflation, future inheritance or endogenous corporate growth. Consumption responds to assumed reason/fear/fantasy coefficients and financial buffers. Saving repays debt first, then adds assets. Illustrative support is $2,000 per represented dependent, capped by donor assets; transfers cancel across groups. Internal child support is not added to national income. This scenario program is separate from the historical calibration and is not a validated psychological predictor.

## Scope and data

EconomyLayouts enables the landscape and preserves the original torus renderers under an unloaded scene id. Swap its two constants to restore the bowl and presets. The existing census, input-output accounts and social seasons remain the data backbone. The complete SPEC2 18-view suite and comprehensive ownership census are not implemented.

Stored economic data predates the September 30, 2026 BEA update; 2026 values are estimates, not a newly researched national account. Named ownership records retain dates and source URLs in hill-owners.json. Psychological and portfolio allocations are model assumptions.

Audio and local tour JSON files and sidecars are ignored and untracked without deleting local copies. Tour source and generation code remain versioned.

## Focus, activity and corporate interiors

Terrain contours are muted below the bloom threshold. Selected and hovered players, selected-sector firms, and the focused ownership rim use HDR highlights. Unrelated activity fades when a player is selected. Click a moving cohort to inspect its accounts; asset and debt rings have logarithmic radii (gold assets, pink liabilities). Marker size is not a linear monetary chart.

Government ownership/agency geometry is below zero at the foundation of the whole landscape. Thin conceptual support connections extend beneath every non-government industry. These are explanatory legal/institutional support paths, not measured fiscal transfers.

The existing sector input-output routes run underground, visible more strongly in the dependency view. Branches from sampled firms to recipient sectors are illustrative allocations; no company-specific supplier evidence is asserted. Employees, management and ownership form schematic interior trees. Same-sector firms have separate positions and rivalry connections.

CorporateCompetition allocates each player's current consumption budget once across sectors using the existing category crosswalk. Where sampled firms exist, a deliberately assumed 50% outside option reserves demand for unlisted suppliers; synthetic appeal/reliability scores split the remaining 50% based on fear. Firm receipts are scenario allocations, not measured market shares or forecasts, and are not added back into household income. The current prototype has demand-side competition, not endogenous prices, bankruptcy or corporate balance sheets.

Player trips are bounded, repeating visual visits toward a deterministic, budget-weighted spending destination. Fear affects travel extent and speed. Movement does not claim geographic migration and does not execute transactions each frame. Each player's annual assets and debt remain in PlayerMindProgram; Step year / Play advances that ledger. River width reflects the displayed sector spending allocation and color reflects the desire/fear balance. Other allocations remain in the conserved corporate budget even when omitted from the overview. This is household demand flowing to business, not an assertion that all spending is investment capital.

Validation additionally checks firm receipts plus the outside option equal total consumption, each player's allocation sums to its budget, and government sits below the terrain. The runtime animation uses a separate layer to avoid rebuilding the terrain every frame.

### Population density and torus river styling

The 116 financial groups are rendered as 580 moving subgroups (five per account), at half the former marker scale. Each subgroup displays one fifth of its parent's assets and debt; the inspector explicitly retains group totals. This is a presentation subdivision, not 580 independently calibrated household agents. Clicking any subgroup selects its financial group. Population weight and ledger balances are not multiplied.

Corporate monogram/ticker badges identify sampled firms; full names and scenario demand appear on focus or in the ownership view. They are procedural identifiers, not official logo assets.

River rendering follows the original FlowsLayer.Ribbons grammar: adjacent saturated fear-blue and desire-pink surface bands, alpha blending to retain hue, a small lane gap, amount-scaled width, and pulsing center lines. The width uses a square-root visual compression for the expanded landscape rather than the torus's linear scale. A subgroup shows a budget-weighted destination; the split lanes divide that allocation by the current assumed fear fraction. Flow animation never adds ledger transactions.

For legibility, the overview draws one in fifteen individual tributaries and all industry trunks. Selecting a financial group reveals its five tributaries. Trunks aggregate all displayed-subgroup allocations for each recipient, not only the sampled tributaries; flows are alternative views of the same money, not additive transactions.

The economy-only bloom profile uses intensity 1.9 and scatter 0.80, keeping the original 0.9 threshold. Moving HDR highlights trace both river lanes; the selected group, large scenario receivers and the dominant demand summit receive stronger focus. The original Why scene retains its existing bloom settings.

## Value creation and capture - 2026-10-03

The landscape now shows where each household dollar goes and who keeps it, end to end.

**The accounting (`ValueCapture`).** Each of the 119 spending items' purchaser-price split across industries
(`spending.json`) runs through the Leontief inverse of the BEA 2024 summary Use table (`circuit.json` io). Each industry's
share of the resulting output splits by its own 2024 accounts into wages (compensation), production taxes, upkeep
(depreciation, the industry's `depShare` of value added) and the owners' net operating surplus (profits, proprietors'
income, rent and net interest). Item import shares leave first. Government surplus is the wear of public capital, so
it counts as upkeep. The kept IO flows are scaled up to each buyer's total intermediate purchases, so imported inputs
count as domestic. The 2024 ratios apply to every year. Of a 2025 household dollar, about 24-31 cents (by category)
becomes owners' surplus. A homeowner's imputed rent is surplus the homeowner owns: it is credited back to that player
and is not "captured". The rest goes to whoever holds claims on the industry: its own business owners (`BusinessBy`)
plus holders of capital income, in proportion to the industry's share of all owners' surplus (a portfolio proxy, not
holdings data). Foreign equity holders get `foreignEquityShare` times the corporate part of surplus.

**How purchases are sold (Jev).** `Tools/judge-purchases.py` asked Jev (TypeSafe System One, `jev-1.13.0`) four Score
questions and one Choice per item: manufactured want, fear sold, captive buyer, habit loop, and the primary motive. The
results are in `purchase-judgments.json`, with the questions, probabilities' summaries and confidence. Persuaded =
1 - (1 - manufactured)(1 - fear); locked in = 1 - (1 - captive)(1 - habit). These are weighted by the owners' surplus
each item carries, per category and per industry. They are model judgments about selling practices, not
measurements of anyone's mind. Several items have low confidence (for example bank fees and foundations). Without the
file, the stored category fear share and item fantasy stand in.

**Socio-economic structure.** Class is altitude. Each cohort sits on its paying hill's contour for its rung
(`groups.json`): out of work on the valley floor; working poor and students in the lower valley; frontline, gig and
Social Security retirees in the foothills; office workers, public servants and comfortable retirees on the slopes; the
professional-managerial class on the upper slopes; business owners just under the summit. The diversified top 1% float
in the clouds. The people view shows a legend of the rungs. The valley-facing front of each hill is left open for the
money.

**The picture.** The desire / fear rivers (rose and ice lanes, from the original torus) run from the households to
each hill's **market** on its valley face. There the money splits: wages run back down to the earners (blue), taxes drop
to the bedrock (steel), upkeep stays (a faint ring), and the owners' surplus climbs on as a **gold river** into the
**gold halo** above the summit. The halo's size is the surplus households' spending generates there. Its rose / ice arcs
show the share Jev judged a manufactured want / a sold fear, and its white ticks the share that is captive or habitual.
Gold threads rise from each halo to the players who collect: owners on the slopes, the clouds, abroad. Purchases between
industries that household spending sets off run underground (amber). Each player carries a ledger ring: gold when it
collects more owners' surplus than its own spending pays, rose when it pays more. Selecting a player draws rose arcs
from its spending into each halo and gold arcs from each halo back to it. The halos, rivers, beams and pillars are drawn
above the 0.9 bloom threshold, and the halos breathe slowly. Investment falls from the cloud pool back onto the halos
(rivers view). The generic hilltop gold bands are gone: every halo is measured.

**Corporate infrastructure (level of detail).** The 25 named firms stand on the far slopes of their hills, not
underground:
- **Far:** a gold beam as tall as the firm's market value (log scale; ticks every $1T), topped by a halo sized by net
  income.
- **Mid** (within 100 units or the focused sector): the revenue ring at the foot with the net margin as a gold arc,
  and a readout of revenue, profit, margin and market value as years of today's profit.
- **Near** (within 35): the organization narrowing up the beam, from the workforce (scaled by US headcount) through
  managers, executives and the board to the owners' halo. The organization is schematic.

**Social infrastructure (level of detail, view 9 "foundation").** A government bedrock lies under the whole landscape.
Its pillars are sized by FY2025 outlays: Social Security ($1.57T, rising to the retirees), Medicare + Medicaid ($1.66T,
under the health hill), defense ($0.87T, under manufacturing and hardware), schools / police / roads / courts (state and
local purchases, $3.31T, under the valley floor), other federal ($1.89T, to the out of work and working poor), and
interest to bondholders ($1.03T, rising to the clouds). Benefits arc from the pillars to every receiving player, and
taxes rain from every household into the bedrock. The bedrock's caption compares corporate tax ($452B) with dividends
and buybacks. Other years scale FY2025 by GDP.

**The simulation closes the loop.** `PlayerMindProgram` now keeps each player's six category budgets:
- **Capture loop.** Each year, last year's spending is traced through `ValueCapture`. Wages and capital income move by
  the change in what households' own spending generated for each player. Transfers and other demand stay at the census
  level.
- **Seller pressure** (a new control, 0-100%). It adds manufactured want (fantasy +0.30) and sold fear (+0.15) to every
  player, lowering saving and tilting budgets toward the categories whose surplus Jev judged most persuaded.
- **Interest.** Borrowing beyond the census debt pays interest at the census rate (personal and mortgage interest over
  household debt). That interest reaches lenders' owners as capital income next year.

The readout shows what a selected player pays owners inside its spending, how much of that is persuaded or locked in,
what it collects, and the net.

**Validation additions.** `HillValidation` now also checks:
- the Leontief inverse (residual < 1e-8) and nonnegative requirements;
- every item's dollar splitting to 100%;
- household spending equal to its parts;
- each market's output equal to household sales plus sales to other industries;
- owners' surplus fully attributed (holders + homeowners + abroad) and wages fully paid;
- the ledger identity with interest;
- seller pressure raising persuaded capture;
- owners and managers sitting above the valley floor of their own hill.

## The economy wave - 2026-10-05

The user's direction: companies have lifespans like people; the economy is a wave moving forward into the future and
creating; the wave is the expanded cross-section of the end of the causality graph; the hierarchy is a hillside where
higher elevation is more influence but depends on the ground below; people inhabit it and their capital and debt flow into
rivers of desire and fear. The road and the landscape now share one time axis.

**One time axis.** The land frame stands on the road's present moment (`EconomyStage.Land`), and the road is 160 units
from 1946 to now (`EconomyStyle.WindowLength`; 16 for the retired bowl), with a taller, wider cross-section for the
lifelines (`YScale` 7, `RhoScale` 5). Land-local z is the road's own time (`HillLandscape.Z` projects the road's position
for each year), so a year on the wave stands beside the same year on the road. The industry wall under the road retires
with the bowl: the wave is that wall unfolded.

**The wave (`HillLandscape`, drawn by `HillWaveLayer`).** Each year's cross-section is a hillside of stacked strata: raw
materials at its foot, then manufacturing and infrastructure, services and tech on top, each tier's layer standing on the
ones beneath, all on the government bedrock. Across the wave, a tier's band is its share of the year's non-government
value added and each industry is a ridge in its tier's band (a 3.6-unit floor on the ridge width keeps small industries
visible; ridges merge by their highest). Risers between tiers stand in their own gaps, so no industry straddles a step.
The cross-section widens with real value added (width ~ real VA^0.5, height ~ real VA^0.3: 88 units of span in 1947,
220 at the 2025 crest). Tier heights (16/24/28/32% of the stack) are a conceptual hierarchy of dependence, not measured
influence. Behind the crest lies the wake: one rib per year (each year's own cross-section), the industries' ridges along
time and the steps between strata. The crest is the shown year; its cut face shows the strata, and the front breaks
forward into the future. The terrain holds the crest's cross-section over its last six units (about three years) so the
present-day hills stand on the shown year's geometry. A year's cross-section does not depend on which crest is shown.

**Class is altitude, on each cohort's own hill.** In a cross-section a downhill neighbor can be a taller ridge, so people,
markets, firm seats and summits stand on their own hill's surface (`Rest`: strata plus that industry's ridge alone), not on
the merged terrain. Rungs run downhill toward the road; cohorts of one rung step back into the recent past. Thin blue
threads fan from the road at the crest year out to every cohort: the population's cross-section, expanded.

**Company lifespans (`CompanyLives`, `company-lives.json`).** 75 US companies: founding year, end year and fate
(bankrupt, acquired, merged, broken up, alive), the industry each stands on, what it sells, and market-value waypoints.
Values are approximate recalled market capitalizations or private valuations in nominal dollars, converted to 2025
dollars with the stored GDP deflator and interpolated geometrically; founding years and fates are the documented ones.
The 25 firms of `circuit.json` end on their stored market value. Each lifeline is born on its ridge (a seed ring), rises
and falls at the height of its market value (the towers' beam scale), and ends: failed lines fall to the ground (a red
cross), bought ones merge into their buyer's line when it is drawn, survivors reach the crest (the 25 stored firms bend
into their tower's halo). Lines follow five-year averages of the ridges so yearly noise in the accounts does not jitter
them. Hover or click a line for its life, peak and selling profile; the "Companies live and die" view (key 0) is a side
view along time.

**Jev judgments of companies (`Tools/judge-companies.py`, `company-judgments.json`).** One request per company with
five independent questions over the same state: which industry earns most of its revenue (a Choice over the 23
non-government industries with a no-match option) and four Scores matching the household purchase judgments
(manufactured want, fear sold, captive buyer, habit loop). `jev-1.13.0`, 147,621 tokens. The line's color is gold where
the company meets needs, tinted rose toward manufactured want and ice toward sold fear by persuaded = 1 - (1 - want)(1 -
fear); lock-in brightens it. Jev's hill replaces the authored industry only at confidence >= 0.6 and only for companies
without a stored tower (towers keep their industry so lifelines land on them): Xerox to computers & chips (0.63), Disney to
arts & entertainment (0.66). Confident disagreements not applied: SpaceX to transport (0.76) and Caterpillar to
construction (0.88; BEA counts machinery as manufacturing). Unsure choices (Enron 0.46, Fannie Mae 0.47, Airbnb 0.36,
Blockbuster 0.53, AOL 0.59, McDonald's 0.56, Nike 0.57) keep the authored industry. These are model judgments about how
companies sell, not measurements.

**Money along time.** In the rivers view each cohort's saving leaves over the crest into the future as gold (capital
that builds what comes next; $1.08T a year in 2025) and its debt arrives from the future as a desire / fear stream (spending
drawn from income not yet earned; $14.35T of household debt), colored by the cohort's motive. Debt is drawn as the stock
owed, not as annual borrowing.

**Clarity.** Every view keeps one story at full strength: the overview and the wave view show the wake, the crest and the
companies; the capture arcs to the clouds, the cloud portfolio threads, the pillars and the benefit arcs are faint outside
their own views. Presets were re-posed for the wave (the "Class is altitude" camera had been inside the bedrock pillars),
the section view is a side view of the road's last decades with the wave behind, and y1972 shows the crest standing back
in 1972 with modern companies unborn.

**Fixes found in review.** The scenario program (`PlayerMindProgram.Active`) is built for one year's cohorts; on a year
change (y1972, the slider) `CorporateCompetition` and `HillActivityLayer` indexed it with another year's cohorts and threw
every frame. They now ignore a program whose cohorts do not match the shown year, as `HillCaptureLayer` already did. And
`HillExplorer` answered every year change by flying to the overview, which undid the y1972 preset (back to 2025) and would
pull the camera on every slider step; it now only flies home when the viewer is inside the explorer.

**Validation additions.** `HillValidation` also checks: the crest's industry bands add up to its value-added width; the
wave's time axis is the road's (a year on the wave sits beside the same year on the road); the wave widens with real
value added; each tier stands on the layers beneath it; nobody stands ahead of the crest; a historical crest conserves
value added and a year's cross-section does not depend on the crest shown; every stored firm has a lifeline; lifelines are
well formed and stand on production ridges; buyers existed when they bought.

## One person in the wave - 2026-10-11

The user's direction: atomistic views of individual humans: where they sit in the social market, how their brain
pursues desire and moves away from fears, how individual and group decisions follow tit-for-tat algorithms (iterated
prisoner's dilemmas), and how an individual moves along the economic ladder, drawn in the landscape. Built as real 3D
structures (rings, spheres of rings, threads with world width) so they read from any side in a headset.

**The followed person (`PersonLife`).** The inspected person (`EconomyState.Person`) when one is selected and an adult
in the shown year; otherwise the first documented notable of `LivesReport` alive then (the striver first, then young,
indebted, owner, escapist, forgiver, heir, avenger, retiree); otherwise the adult whose household income rank rose most
over ten years. Their adult records up to the shown year, their spouse each year (MarriageLog) and their household's
hill: their own industry when employed, else the employed spouse's (the census's dominance rule), else none. Events read
off the records: start, first job, industry change, job loss, marriage and its end, children, first home, own business,
gaining control of their path, debt peak, inheritance, discharge, retirement (at most 16).

**Where a person stands (`HillLandscape.Rung`).** Class is altitude, as for the cohorts: on the household's hill at the
rung of household income rank (`IncomeRank x 4`, business owners one rung up, interpolated over `RungRadius`), on the
valley side, at the year's z on the road's time axis; government-paid or without a hill, on the valley floor.

**Atom (`HillPersonLayer`, view `atom`, anchors `atom:person`, `atom:mind`).** A figure at that place (gold when in
control; gold and rose rings for net worth and debt), and above it the mind (`MindDiagram`, which also replaces the
six-node diagram of the mind view, key 7, for a selected individual or cohort): the lives model's drive weights for the
year (`EconomicLives.DriveWeights`: food, collective, law, shelter, sex; starvation, isolation, murder, exposure,
childless), each a sphere sized by its share of the motive (weight x desire or fear share), threads into a deliberation
gate whose aperture is reason (future and fantasy named), a choice (spend in the motive's color, save gold, borrow rose,
as shares of disposable income) and memory (net worth, debt, runway) feeding back into the fears. In the landscape:
rose threads from the person to the market serving each desire (assumed from psyche.json's "served by": restaurants,
entertainment, legal, real estate, retail trade), ice threads from the threat each fear moves away from (losing the job
at the employer's market, platforms, police and courts, health care, personal services), plus debt arriving from the
future and a thin buffer, each ending in a push away from the threat at the person; income threads arrive from the
employer (nearest stored firm on the hill, wages blue, own business gold), the government bedrock (pillar), the clouds
(capital income), a supporting parent and inheritances. The figure sways along the net pull. The drive-to-market
mapping and the sway are illustrations, not measured behavior.

**Dilemma (`DilemmaArena`, `HillDilemmaLayer`, view `dilemma`, anchors `dilemma:arena`, `dilemma:groups`).** An arena
beside the person. Counterparts are real records of the same year, picked deterministically: the spouse (else a parent),
a coworker (same industry), a neighbor (same cohort), a boss or firm (a self-employed high earner of the industry) and
someone of the other tribe; each plays its `PersonTraits.Strategy`. A real iterated prisoner's dilemma with pairwise
memory (`PrisonersDilemma.Intend`, games.json payoffs), one round per 0.6 s, 36-round matches with a fixed seed and no
noise; in round 9 the person defects once against the coworker, so retaliation, the echo of two tit-for-tat players, or
forgiveness is legible. Pulses along each link (blue cooperate, red defect), strips of the last 12 rounds per partner
(gold diagonals where the partner copied the person's previous move), payoff bars (gold) against mutual defection (grey).
The ledger turns the cooperation dividend over mutual defection into an on-screen sum (1 point = 0.02% of disposable
income, illustrative; no historical record or scenario ledger changes). Above, the group level: the year's
`SocialSeason` (mean field over cohort shares) at the season round matching the arena's clock: a ring for the person's
tribe and one for the other with in-tribe cooperation as gold arcs, a bridge colored by cross-tribe cooperation.

**Ladder (`HillPersonLayer`, view `ladder`, anchor `ladder:path`).** The adult life across the wave: each year at
`Rung` of that year (z = the year on the road's axis), a line from labor blue to capital gold with agency (gold when in
control), width by net worth; moves to another hill arc over the ground; event markers with short labels; a faint white
ghost at the parent's earnings rank (`ParentRank`) on the same hills; a walker repeats the life in a loop with its year,
age, rank and net worth.

**Presets.** `atom`, `dilemma` and `ladder` are keyless; `HillPresets.Person` poses them on where the person stands and
`RefreshPersonViews` updates the catalog's objects in place (and the camera when one is on screen) when the person or
the year changes. `EconomyViews.Get` now gives an id without a column its own spec (a copy of the overview's) instead of
the overview itself, so `LandView.PresetId` names these views and `companies` (which had silently read as `overview`).

**Fixes in this round.**
- *Scenario fear never evolved.* `PlayerMindProgram.Step` computed the year's fear and discarded it. Fear and reason now
  relax a third of the way per year (half-life about 1.7 years) toward a target: the disposition plus stress (a thin
  buffer, debt service, income lost against the census) and seller pressure; stress lowers reason. A control's
  adjustment moves the disposition, so it persists. Coefficients are assumptions.
- *Government-paid cohorts were inside the bedrock.* Public servants, Social Security retirees and the out of work on
  the safety net are anchored to the government hills (y -18). They now stand on the valley floor between the road and
  the hillside's foot near the crest, lower rungs nearer the road, each government in its own stretch; the government's
  market stands there too, actors keep their footing, and the people view's legend names them. HillValidation checks it.
- *Golden circles.* Every stored firm sits inside a gold ring of owner figures around its profit halo, sized by the top
  1%'s DFA share of corporate equity times the domestic part of its market value, with a thin gold thread to the 1%
  cloud. A portfolio proxy, not a register; the two dated SEC voting-control rings remain.
- *River color.* Lanes were split by the spender's average fear. They now follow the fear share of what the budget buys
  at that market (`ValueCapture.SoldFear`: Jev's fear sold / (fear sold + manufactured want) per item, shrunk toward the
  category's stored fear share with a prior of 0.2, weighted like `Category.IndustryShare`): health 0.85 and insurance
  0.70 read as ice, entertainment and restaurants 0.21 as rose. Markers keep the spender's fear.

Needs a check in the Editor: the poses and label sizes of the three views, overlaps between pull labels at shared
markets, and the arena's height over steep valley slopes.

## Popular people and the government - 2026-10-13

**The social layer's data** (`Tools/social`, output `Assets/Resources/Data/social/people.json`):
- **Source.** Built from the social graph (`SocialGraph/global_social_graph.db`, the social-graph + GPU-FDG pipeline behind
  Unity-FDG): 1,139 Instagram accounts with follower counts, follow ties, category clouds and force-directed positions.
- **Stage 1, `facts.py` (Wikidata).** Matches each account to Wikidata by its Instagram username (P2003; 958 exact) or by
  name search.
- **Stage 2, `judge.py` (Jev).** Asks only what the facts leave open:
  - which candidate owns the account, when the handle did not match or the matched name disagrees;
  - person / group / organization;
  - region, when citizenship does not settle it;
  - political role, for political accounts;
  - whether a business person controls a large company and, if so, which of the 49 living economy companies (a single
    choice whose probability spread names every company it weights, e.g. Musk: Tesla and SpaceX);
  - birth decade, when Wikidata has no birth year.
  This took 710 requests.
- **Stage 3, `export.py` (code-owned policy).** Keeps 1,074 people and groups (65 brands and outlets dropped).
  - *Region:* a lone citizenship region beats Jev; K-pop and Bollywood members belong to East and South Asia.
  - *Offices:* government offices only (no goodwill ambassadors, "-elect" titles or company titles).
  - *Layout:* each region's FDG positions centered and scaled to unit radius.
  - *Regions:* US 365, East Asia 122, South Asia 100, Europe 174, rest of world 313.
  - *Followers:* follower counts are a 2025 snapshot.

**Causality graph:**
- **Lifelines.** `PopularLines` (built with the figures in `LifelineBuild`) draws every popular person's lifeline from
  birth to death or now, with the figures' status curve and white tint.
  - *Placement:* in their region's band — the US stream (the SMV crowd's envelope); India; China + Japan; the European
    streams; or a band just outside every stream for the rest of the world.

## The wave replayed - 2026-10-22

The user's direction: a replay of the wave, the crest advancing year by year from the first year to the latest, so cause
and effect plays out instead of being read off; a readout of what changed each year from data already in the project;
a handful of turning points read off the data, not authored; every view kept working.

**The replay (`EconomyReplay`, `ReplayPanel`).** R plays or pauses it (Shift+R from the start); so do the play button on
the year chip and, in the headset, a replay button beside the other scene and recenter in the hand menu. It starts at
1950 when the crest stands at the present (the default year or later), else from the year the viewer chose, and stops at
the latest year (2026, an estimate). The land cannot open before 1950 (`EconomyState.MinYear`: the simulated population
begins then), so the ribs of 1947-49 already lie in the wake at the start. One year at a time:
- *No pile-up.* `EconomyState.Year` moves only once `LandService` has put the year on screen (`Current.Year` and
  `ShownYear`, i.e. every land layer swapped to it). While a year is shown the next year's snapshot is built on a worker
  into the service's cache (`LandService.BuildBlocking(int)`), and the step waits for that build, so the request that
  follows is answered from the cache: at most one land build is ever in flight.
- *Pace.* About a second a year from the request (`YearSeconds`), `QuietSeconds` (0.6 s) through quiet years (no rate,
  tariff, outlay or tier-share move of a point, the market grew, no stored company born or ended), and `TurnSeconds`
  (2.6 s, counted from when the year shows) at a turning point. 1950-2026 runs in about 90 s.
- *Who owns the year.* Moving the year by hand (keys, the chip, the scrubber, a view with a year of its own such as y1972)
  pauses the replay; the guided tour stops it. A year that is not on screen 20 s after it was asked for logs an error and
  pauses (`StallSeconds`): the smoke test fails on it.

**The readout (`ReplayFacts`).** A caption at the bottom centre above the preset bar (in portrait under the title and the
year chip), kept in the headset (the canvas is one of `XrSession`'s kept canvases). From the stored series only:
- GDP (nominal) and its real growth (deflated by the stored GDP price index), and the market's real value added - every
  industry but government, deflated: the wave's own width - with "the wave narrows" when it falls;
- the four market tiers' shares of the market's value added and their moves of half a point or more;
- the federal funds and effective tariff rates (`money-and-trade.json`, annual averages; the funds rate from 1955) with
  their change in points; federal outlays and receipts as shares of GDP (`government.json`, OMB fiscal years);
- the stored companies founded and ended that year (`company-lives.json`, with their fate);
- in the mobility view, the decade's ladder of `HillMobilityLayer.Shown`: the shares of adults who climbed a fifth or more
  and who fell, and the like-for-like stays of the bottom and top fifths.

**Turning points, read off the data.** Fixed rules over 1947-2026, so a revised series moves them:
- *recessions:* runs of years in which the market's real value added fell, ranked by the run's whole fall; the four deepest
  are kept, marked at the run's last year (1958 -1.6%, 1982 -2.6%, 2008-09 -3.7%, 2020 -2.6%). Every other fall (1949,
  1954, 1970, 1974-75, 1980, 1991) still shows in its year's line as the wave narrowing;
- *rate swings:* runs of annual federal funds averages moving one way, ranked by their size in points; the four largest
  are kept, marked where they turn (peaks 1974 at 10.5% and 1981 at 16.4%, troughs 1983 at 9.1% and 1993 at 3.0%);
- *the tariff rate:* its largest run (2.6% in 2024 to 8.8% in 2026, an H1-annualized estimate gross of the IEEPA refunds).
They are ticked on the year chip's scrubber (rose recession, gold rate, steel tariff; hovering a tick reads it), the
replay holds at each with the turning point's sentence in its colour, and once the crest has passed one its year's rib is
traced faintly in that colour in the wake with a pin rising from the rib's highest ridge to its tag (`HillReplayLayer`,
during the replay in the overview, landscape and companies views; the rib the replay holds on pulses), so the wave keeps
the history it has just been through without drowning the companies' lines that run along it.

**Every view keeps working.** The views framed on the crest (landscape, capture, people, rivers, society, betrayal,
foundation, social, roots, strategy, mobility) follow it: when the land of a new year is shown and the catalog is refreshed,
the move of the view's own target is added to the camera's target and eased in (0.3 s), after any flight on its way, so
the viewer's orbit, zoom and pan stay theirs. The section view (its cut), the followed person's views and the viewer's
place keep their own camera logic. The views of the whole time axis - the overview, the companies' lives and the money
chart - now frame the wake up to the present whatever year the crest stands at (`HillPresets`: the axis end is the later
of the shown year and the default year), so the wave grows across them during the replay instead of the camera being
carried into the empty past before the first rib; before, pressing 1 at an early year framed mostly the years before 1947.
The money chart already draws only up to the crest, so its curves draw themselves as the replay advances; its openness
label now quotes the crest's year instead of always the latest. In the mobility view the readout leaves out the tiers and
companies, so the ladder's own labels beneath it stay clear.

**Checks.** `ReplayFacts.Problems` runs where the facts are built (an error fails the smoke test): every year has a GDP and
