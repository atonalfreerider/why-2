## What the research is for

The app is a Unity simulation. Its economy is a 3D landscape behind a "causality timeline" (a wave of history running
up to the present). The user keeps the timeline. The redesign rebuilds the **2026 cross-section**, opened into a full 3D
hillside, around competitive advantage:

- **Actors** (companies, labs, states, investors, armies, species) are objects. Size shows scale (market cap or
  valuation, assets). **Altitude shows dominance**: the higher an actor stands, the more of the economy's surplus its
  advantage lets it keep. Supply-chain position sets depth (upstream to downstream); rivals stand side by side.
- **Rivers** carry money between actors: revenue, cost, capex and capital. They are coloured by motive: **desire**
  (buying to win) or **fear** (spending so as not to die). Where many rivers must pass through one actor, that actor is
  a **choke point**.
- **Flashpoints** are sparks between actors where an advantage decides a contest: one per decisive episode, in 2026
  and back through history.
- **Scenarios** change the environment (friendly or unfriendly to each player), and the landscape shows who rises and
  who falls: a strategist's prediction, drawn from competitive position.

Your research supplies the sourced facts, the actors, the flows, the choke points, the flashpoints and the lineage
behind all of this, plus a written recursive analysis of your part.

## How to think

Think like a strategist who has to bet money on the outcome. For every important actor ask, recursively:

1. **What is its advantage, exactly?** Name the mechanism, not a slogan. Use Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers for firms
   (scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process
   power) and the user's triad for everything: **information** (knowing more, sooner: research, data, talent,
   intelligence), **execution** (doing it better: process, organisation, speed, tactics) and **environment** (being in
   the right place: geography, regulation, capital, timing, position in a supply chain).
2. **Whom did it beat, and how?** That episode is a flashpoint: holder, rivals, date, mechanism, outcome.
3. **What does it stand on?** Its suppliers, its customers, its government, its IP protection, its capital. Then recurse
   into the actors it stands on, and back to the actors that made it possible (spin-offs, technology transfers, people).
4. **What does it desire, and what does it fear?** What is it racing toward, and what would kill it? Use the actors'
   own words where they have said it on the record (earnings calls, letters, interviews).
5. **What would change the outcome?** Which exogenous conditions (energy, Taiwan, export controls, interest rates,
   regulation, a cheaper model) would strengthen or weaken it?

Be accurate even where the request simplifies. Where the sources disagree with a premise (for example, stirrups were
not unique to the Mongols), record what the sources say, and state the stronger version of the user's point (the
Mongols' advantage was a system: stirrup and composite bow, several horses per rider, decimal organisation, the
absorption of defeated troops). Never invent a number, a date or a quote.

## The rules

1. **Every number is a fact with a source.** A fact must pass the truth gate (below). Numbers you cannot verify stay
   out of the data; mention them in your notes as unverified if they matter.
2. **Quotes are whole sentences** (or whole table rows) copied from the fetched text, normally 10 to 60 words,
   containing every value of the fact. A one-word quote is not acceptable. Quote as little as you need: these are short
   excerpts for verification, never long passages.
3. **2026 facts** (market caps, valuations, 2026 revenue, 2026 deals, current shares) set `"dated2026": true`. The gate
   re-reads their source today, so the quote must be on the live page today.
4. **Sources**, best first: company investor-relations releases and filings, annual reports, official statistics
   (BEA, Census, IEA, EIA, BIS, the Federal Register), the original papers (arXiv), reputable press that a script can
   read (Reuters, AP, CNBC, The Verge, Ars Technica, company blogs), then Wikipedia for history (cite the sentence).
   Many sites refuse scripts (Bloomberg, WSJ, FT, some IR sites behind Cloudflare). If a page will not fetch, use
   another source; try `--browser` once.
5. **People.** Name people only where they are part of the mechanism (a founder, a defector, a general). The app shows
   images only of three approved people; that does not limit your text.
6. **Ids.** Fact ids start with your cluster name (`compute-nvidia-latest-quarter-revenue`). Actor ids are lowercase with
   hyphens; use the canonical ids below and add new ones only when needed.
7. **No credentials** anywhere: not in files, not in commands you print. Jev is reached only through the gate.
8. **Write early.** Write your JSON file and your Markdown file as soon as you have a first version, then extend them,
   so a usage limit never loses your work.
9. **Economy.** Fetch a page once (the helper caches it), then search it with `grep`/`find`; never print a whole page
   into your context. Aim for **30 to 45 verified facts** and stop searching when your cluster's core is covered.
   Spend at most about 45 fetches.

## Tools

From the repository root `/home/john/Desktop/Unity/why-2`:

- Find sources with the WebSearch tool (load it with ToolSearch, query `select:WebSearch`, if it is not loaded).
- Fetch and search with the helper (cache in `~/Desktop/Unity/.why-2-build/work/advantage/cache/`):

      H=~/Desktop/Unity/.why-2-build/work/advantage/bin/src.py
      python3 -I $H get NAME URL [--browser]      # fetch once into the cache; prints status and size
      python3 -I $H grep NAME 'REGEX'             # matching lines of the page's text, with context
      python3 -I $H find NAME "EXACT QUOTE"       # is this exact quote in the page's text (as the gate normalises it)?
      python3 -I $H text NAME | sed -n 1,80p      # the page's text, a slice at a time

- The truth gate, on your cluster only (writes `Docs/research/advantage/gate/<cluster>.json`):

      python3 -I Tools/advantage/verify.py --cluster <cluster>

  It fetches each fact's `url` itself (with its own cache), checks that every quote occurs verbatim in the page's
  text (whitespace collapsed, curly quotes straightened, nothing else), that every value occurs inside the quotes, and
  asks Jev (TypeSafe's System One model) whether the text around the quotes supports the claim; a fact passes at a
  probability of 0.6 or more. Run it after your first ten facts, then again at the end, and fix what fails: a quote
  not found (copy it again from `text`; PDFs may break lines, so pick another sentence), a value not in the quote
  (write the value exactly as the quote writes it: "130.5", "$4.5 trillion" -> value "4.5"), a low Jev score (make the
  claim say only what the quote says), a fetch failure (another source). Facts that still fail after one fix: delete
  them or move them to your notes. Report your final verified count.
