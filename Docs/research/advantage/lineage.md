# Lineage: from the transistor to the 2026 AI economy

Cluster `lineage`, as of 7 Nov 2026. History only; the one present-tense fact is NVIDIA's position.

## The spine

1. **Bell Labs, 1947.** Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley invented the transistor [lineage-bell-transistor-1947] and shared the 1956 Nobel Prize [lineage-nobel-1956]. The advantage was a cornered resource: a protected monopoly's research budget. It did not stay cornered. Bell Labs' discovery of oxide passivation reached Shockley's staff as a preprint in December 1956 [lineage-bell-passivation-preprint-1956].
2. **Shockley Semiconductor, 1955.** Founded with Beckman Instruments' money [lineage-shockley-lab-1955]. It collected talent and could not hold it.
3. **Fairchild, 1957.** Eight engineers left and formed Fairchild [lineage-traitorous-eight-names][lineage-fairchild-founded-1957]. Hoerni's planar process (1959) [lineage-fairchild-planar-1959] let Noyce make the first monolithic integrated circuit [lineage-noyce-monolithic-ic-1959]. Kilby's Texas Instruments design shared the invention and won a Nobel Prize in 2000 [lineage-kilby-nobel-2000], but the planar route was the manufacturable one. First orders came from IBM, which bought the first 100 devices at $150 each for a bomber's computer [lineage-fairchild-first-batch-ibm]. The mechanism is process power. Its people were its real export: by 1960 it was the incubator of dozens of firms, Intel and AMD among them [lineage-fairchildren-1960].
4. **Intel, 1968.** Founded by Moore and Noyce with Arthur Rock [lineage-intel-founded-1968]; Faggin followed in 1970 [lineage-faggin-left-fairchild-1970]. Intel's 1103 was the first commercial DRAM (1970) [lineage-intel-1103-dram-1970] and the best-selling memory chip by 1972 [lineage-intel-1103-bestseller-1972]. The 4004 microprocessor came in 1971 from a Busicom calculator job and did not become the core business until the mid-1980s [lineage-intel-4004-1971]. In 1983 Japanese memory makers squeezed profits and Andy Grove moved Intel to microprocessors [lineage-intel-japan-dram-1983]. The IBM PC (1981) used the 8088 [lineage-ibm-pc-8088-1981], and Compaq's 386 machine (1986) beat IBM to the next standard and made Intel the key supplier [lineage-compaq-386-1986]. The advantage was switching costs: software built on x86.
5. **TSMC, 1987.** A joint venture of Taiwan's government, ITRI and private investors, and the first dedicated foundry [lineage-tsmc-founded-1987]. Competing with no customer let fabless firms exist; today it holds about 70% of the foundry market [lineage-tsmc-foundry-share-2026] and was first to run EUV in volume [lineage-tsmc-first-euv-volume]. Intel, which once owned the process lead, now has the US government as a 9.9% owner [lineage-us-gov-intel-stake-2025].
6. **ASML.** EUV development began in 1997 [lineage-asml-euv-1997]. In 2012 Intel (with $4.1 billion for 15 percent [lineage-intel-asml-4-1bn-2012]), TSMC and Samsung bought 23 percent of ASML to pay for it [lineage-asml-coinvest-2012]. As of 2026 it is the only EUV maker [lineage-asml-only-euv-2026]. The customers created their own choke point.
7. **NVIDIA, 1993.** Founded by Huang, Malachowsky and Priem [lineage-nvidia-founded-1993]; Huang had started at AMD, a Fairchild offspring [lineage-huang-amd-first-job]. Sega's $5 million kept it alive after a failed contract [lineage-sega-invest-5m]; Sega later sold its stake for $15 million [lineage-sega-sold-15m]. It went public in January 1999 [lineage-nvidia-ipo-1999] and shipped the GeForce 256, the first "GPU" [lineage-geforce-256-1999]. In December 2000 it agreed to buy its fallen rival 3dfx's intellectual assets [lineage-nvidia-acquires-3dfx-2000]. CUDA (created 2004, released 2007) opened the chip to general computation [lineage-cuda-released-2007].
8. **AlexNet, 2012.** Trained for five to six days on two GTX 580s [lineage-alexnet-two-gtx580-2012], cards that sold for $500 [lineage-gtx580-price-500]. The gaming market had paid for the hardware and CUDA was ready.
9. **The Transformer, 2017.** A public paper from Google researchers [lineage-transformer-google-2017]; 41.8 BLEU after 3.5 days on eight GPUs [lineage-attention-bleu-8gpu]. Dropping recurrence made training parallel, so scale became the lever. NVIDIA handed OpenAI its first DGX-1 in August 2016 [lineage-dgx1-openai-2016] and bought Mellanox for $6.9 billion in 2019 [lineage-mellanox-6-9bn-2019] to own the network between GPUs.
10. **Scaling and the product.** Loss falls as a power law in model size, data and compute across more than seven orders of magnitude [lineage-scaling-laws-2020]; GPT-3 had 175 billion parameters [lineage-gpt3-175b-2020]; ChatGPT launched on 30 Nov 2022 [lineage-chatgpt-release-2022] and reached 900 million weekly users by February 2026 [lineage-chatgpt-users-2026]. Eleven OpenAI employees left to found Anthropic [lineage-anthropic-founders-from-openai]. The Hutter Prize organizers hold that compression and AI are equivalent problems [lineage-hutter-compression-ai]; that is their view, not a result, and the gate found no OpenAI source for the phrase.
11. **Now.** NVIDIA is worth $4.506 trillion and ranks second in the world [lineage-nvidia-marketcap-now], after being the first to $5 trillion in 2025 [lineage-nvidia-5tn-2025], and it took 180 days to go from $1 to $2 trillion where Apple and Microsoft needed over 500 [lineage-nvidia-2tn-180-days]. It held 92% of discrete desktop and laptop GPUs in Q1 2025 [lineage-nvidia-gpu-share-2025] (a PC-GPU share, not an AI-accelerator share).

## Patterns

- **Advantage leaks through people and publications.** Bell Labs to Shockley to Fairchild to Intel and AMD to NVIDIA's founder; the Transformer paper was public; OpenAI's alumni founded Anthropic. The holder of a technology rarely keeps it; the holder of the manufacturing process and the installed base does.
- **Standards beat inventions.** Bell Labs invented the transistor, Fairchild made it manufacturable, Intel got the standard (x86), NVIDIA got the software (CUDA). Each time, the winner sat one layer above the invention.
- **Customers fund their own choke points.** Intel, TSMC and Samsung capitalised ASML; the gaming market paid for AI hardware.
- **Dominant firms fall when the process or market shifts under them**: Fairchild's people left; Intel's memory business lost to Japan and its fabs to the foundry model.

## What a strategist would predict

- NVIDIA's moat is software switching costs on top of TSMC and ASML, two choke points it does not control. A strategist bets against it only if a cheaper route to training or inference appears, or if Taiwan or export controls hit; the history says the next shift will arrive as a public paper or a spin-off team, not as a rival's product launch.
- ASML and TSMC are the deepest choke points because the customers' own money built them and nothing replaces them before a decade of work.
- Intel's state ownership suggests that governments will treat the chain as strategic infrastructure.
- Labs like OpenAI and Anthropic stand on NVIDIA; their talent is portable, as the 2020-21 split shows.
