# Materials and government: the two deepest strata

Today is 7 November 2026. Facts here passed the truth gate (70 of 70). Ids in brackets.

## What the AI chain stands on

**Rare earths and magnets.** The IEA's July 2026 outlook puts China at about 60% of mined magnet rare earths, over 90% of their refining and nearly 95% of permanent-magnet production [materials-china-magnet-re-share]. BOFIT gives 84% refining, down from over 90% [materials-bofit-refining-84]. Heavy rare earths are tighter: roughly 99% of processing capacity [materials-hree-99-processing]. The US still took 71% of its rare-earth compound and metal imports from China in 2021-24 [materials-usgs-re-import-china]. Outside China, projects will cover only about half of mining needs, a quarter of refining demand and under a fifth of magnet demand by 2035 [materials-iea-2035-outside-china]; the IEA says $60 billion is needed to diversify [materials-iea-60bn-diversify].

**Silicon, gallium, germanium.** China made almost 80% of world silicon materials in 2025 [materials-usgs-si-china-80]. Gallium (73% of US use is integrated circuits [materials-usgs-ga-ic-use]) and germanium are controlled by Beijing: US germanium metal imports fell 67% in 2025 to 7,000 kg after China's 2024 ban [materials-usgs-ge-import-drop]. China made 99% of primary low-purity gallium in 2025 [materials-usgs-ga-china-99], so gallium is a near-total chokepoint, though China supplied only 18% of US gallium metal imports [materials-usgs-ga-import-china]. A world germanium share could not be verified. Wafers: one 2026 industry report, citing SEMI for the market size, puts Shin-Etsu at about 28% [materials-wafer-share-shin-etsu], SUMCO 25% [materials-wafer-share-sumco] and GlobalWafers 15% [materials-wafer-share-globalwafers], with Siltronic 12% and SK Siltron 11%; five firms hold about 91%. That is a single secondary source, so treat it as indicative. Market caps: Shin-Etsu $61.01bn [materials-mcap-shin-etsu], GlobalWafers $8.32bn [materials-mcap-globalwafers], SUMCO $3.94bn [materials-mcap-sumco]: control of wafers is not sized by market value.

**Copper and uranium.** Data centres embody roughly 300,000 to 700,000 tonnes of copper in 2026 [materials-kpler-dc-copper], but that is under 2% of 28.7 million tonnes of world refined use [materials-kpler-dc-share]; the shock is supply. Grasberg, about 3% of world copper, is under force majeure with close to 600,000 tonnes lost to end-2026 [materials-kpler-grasberg]. Freeport is worth $85.32bn [materials-mcap-freeport-mcmoran], Southern Copper $142.73bn, Rio Tinto $136.18bn. USGS puts 2025 world copper mine output at 23,000 kt, led by Chile (5,300), Congo (3,200) and Peru (2,700), with China at 1,800 [materials-usgs-cu-mine-2025], but China refined 14,000 of 29,000 kt, about 48% [materials-usgs-cu-refinery-china-2025]. For nuclear power, Kazatomprom itself mined 12,463 tonnes in 2024, 21% of the world, ahead of Cameco (17%) and Orano (11%) [materials-wna-kazatomprom-21]. Kazakhstan the country mined 39%, Canada 24%, Namibia 12% [materials-wna-uranium-2024]; the country figure is nearly double the company's own share. Kazatomprom is $15.44bn, Cameco $31.05bn.

## The flashpoints

1. **April 2025:** China licensed samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium [materials-china-apr-2025-controls]; **October 2025:** five more [materials-china-oct-2025-controls]; **November 2025:** suspended for a year [materials-china-nov-2025-suspension], to 10 November 2026 [materials-china-suspension-window], three days from now. The gallium/germanium/antimony ban on the US is suspended to 27 November 2026 [materials-china-ga-ge-ban-suspension]. The US Affiliates Rule returns after 10 November [materials-us-affiliates-rule-returns].
2. **July 2025:** the Pentagon invested $400 million in MP Materials [materials-dod-mp-400m], got a warrant to 15% [materials-dod-mp-warrant], guaranteed $110/kg NdPr for ten years [materials-dod-mp-floor] and 100% offtake of the 10X magnets [materials-dod-mp-offtake]. In June 2026 China put MP Materials on its export-control list [materials-china-listed-mp-materials]. MP is worth $6.30bn [materials-mcap-mp-materials].
3. **April 2025 to January 2026, the export-control regime:** on 15 April 2025 Nvidia said it would take a charge of about $5.5bn [materials-h20-5-5bn-charge]; the charge actually booked in Q1 FY2026 was $4.5bn [materials-nvidia-h20-charge-4-5bn]. In August 2025 AP reported, from a US official, that Nvidia and AMD agreed to share 15% of China chip revenue with the government [materials-h20-15pct-ap]; Trump later wrote the cut would be 25% for approved chips [materials-h200-25pct-cut]. The administration approved H200 sales [str-h200-approval], yet Chinese customs were told to block them [compute-china-h200-customs] and NVIDIA's China share fell to zero [str-nvidia-china-zero]. In the Netherlands, since 7 September 2024 ASML needs a Dutch licence, not a US one, for its 1970i and 1980i DUV systems [materials-asml-dutch-duv-licence].
4. **October-November 2025:** the Dutch order on Nexperia, suspended not cancelled [materials-nexperia-suspended].

## Government as a stratum

The state is a shareholder and a toll-gate. The US took 9.9% of Intel, $8.9bn at $20.47 a share [materials-us-intel-stake-89], paid for by $5.7bn of unpaid CHIPS grants and $3.2bn of Secure Enclave awards [materials-us-intel-stake-funding]; the CHIPS Act offered $39bn of subsidies [materials-chips-act-39bn]. A 25% Section 232 tariff on some advanced AI chips began 15 January 2026 [materials-sec232-25pct]. The EU approved only EUR 13.75bn of State aid [materials-eu-chips-state-aid] on a EUR 42bn programme [materials-eu-chips-42bn]. Japan's Rapidus funding is 424.95bn yen [materials-rapidus-424]; a plan reported in 2025 lifts government support to 2.9tn yen [materials-rapidus-2.9tn].

### Taiwan: the state that hosts the chokepoint

Taiwan's National Development Fund holds 6.38% of TSMC, the second-largest holder [materials-taiwan-ndf-tsmc-638], and TSMC held 72.5% of top-10 foundry revenue in Q2 2026 [compute-tsmc-foundry-share]. Taiwan's N-1 rule will apply to TSMC's US fabs: only a process one generation older may be deployed abroad [materials-taiwan-n1-rule]. In January 2026 the US cut tariffs on most Taiwanese goods from 20% to 15% [materials-taiwan-us-tariff-15], and Taiwanese firms are set to channel US$250 billion into US output [materials-taiwan-us-250bn]. Taiwan also took 32% of ASML's Q3 2026 equipment sales [str-asml-q3-regions]. The state's real power is the rule about where the newest node may be built.

## What a strategist would act on

- The chokepoint is processing, not mining; China's share falls slowly (90% to 84%) while heavy rare earths stay near total.
- 10 and 27 November 2026 are the dates: extension, selective reinstatement or full return are the three branches.
- State capital is now a moat: MP Materials and Intel survive on public price risk. Their downside is political (Congress funding, a floor).
- Copper is a supply story; AI is a small slice of demand. Mines sit in Chile, Congo and Peru, but refining sits in China.
- Count the state's strings, not its cash: the 25% and 15% revenue shares, the N-1 rule and the Dutch licence decide who may sell or build what; subsidies are one-off cheques of $5bn to $7bn.
- Control share differs from country share: Kazatomprom has 21%, Kazakhstan 39%; Shin-Etsu is 28% of wafers at about 15 times the market cap of SUMCO, which holds 25%.

Unverified, left out: germanium's world production share, Taiwan's share of advanced packaging and leading-edge capacity, BHP and Codelco shares, helium and neon, Big Fund III's shareholders, and Bloomberg's 476bn yen for the first Kumamoto fab.
