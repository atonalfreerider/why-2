## French support

France did not win the war by sending a large army: Rochambeau's expeditionary force was 6,000 troops [story-revolutions-rochambeau-1780], and the combined Franco-American army at Yorktown about 19,000 [story-revolutions-yorktown-allied-army]. What France held was **supply and the sea**. Vergennes secretly funded a front company to buy munitions [story-revolutions-vergennes-secret-funds]; the treaty of alliance followed Saratoga, where Burgoyne's 5,791 men surrendered to about 12,000 [story-revolutions-saratoga-1777], in February 1778 [story-revolutions-alliance-1778]. The Treaty of Paris was signed on 3 September 1783 [story-revolutions-treaty-paris-1783].

The strategist's point: the Americans won a military contest and France won a political one, and then France paid for it. The advantage that beat Britain was the same capital that, five years later, could not be raised.

## The monarchy's collapse

The crown could not tax the privileged and could not stop borrowing. Royal household spending was 13% of state expenses in 1788 [story-revolutions-royal-household-1788]: the symbol was larger than the cost, but interest was half of revenue. The First Estate, 115,000 to 150,000 clergy [story-revolutions-clergy-1789], was under 1 percent of the population; the Third Estate, with 98 percent, was given double representation. The Assembly turned the Church's land into the backing for the assignats [story-revolutions-church-property-1789] and by the Civil Constitution cut 135 bishops to 83 [story-revolutions-civil-constitution-bishops]. The annual deficit rose from 10% to 64% of gross national product between 1789 and 1793 [story-revolutions-deficit-1789-1793].

## Revolution and tyranny

The Republic's advantage was **no head of state appointed by God**. That let it do what a monarchy could not: execute its king [story-revolutions-louis-executed] and its queen [story-revolutions-marie-antoinette-executed], conscript a nation, and answer the First Coalition of 1793 [story-revolutions-first-coalition-1793]. The cost was the Terror: the Law of Suspects [story-revolutions-law-of-suspects], 16,594 official death sentences [story-revolutions-terror-death-sentences], Robespierre and twenty-one others sentenced without a hearing [story-revolutions-robespierre-executed].

## Napoleon and the return of a divine emperor

Napoleon inherited the nation in arms and added **command** (one commander, six corps, massed artillery [archetypes-napoleon-grande-armee-corps] [archetypes-napoleon-massed-artillery]) and, after the Concordat, **legitimacy**. The Senate vested the government in an emperor on 18 May 1804 [archetypes-napoleon-emperor-1804]; the Grande Armée was formed that year from an Army of England of over 100,000 men [story-revolutions-grande-armee-1804]. At the coronation the Pope was present, in Notre-Dame, but Napoleon decided to crown himself and omitted the formula showing a monarch receiving his crown from the Church [archetypes-napoleon-crowned-himself] [story-revolutions-coronation-ceremony]. Britain, Russia, Sweden and the Ottoman Empire refused to recognize the title; Austria accepted it [story-revolutions-recognition-1804]. The paradox the user names is the data: a regime founded on the end of divine right ended by crowning itself with divine witness.

## Europe out of balance, and the alliance

France had 28 million people against Britain's 12 million and Russia's 35 to 40 million [story-revolutions-europe-populations-1790s]; 2.1 million were conscripted between 1805 and 1813 [story-revolutions-conscripts-1805-1813]. The Continental System embargoed Britain from 21 November 1806 [story-revolutions-berlin-decree], but the loss of trade hit France and its allies and led to the weakening of Napoleon's coalition [story-revolutions-system-weakened-coalition]. Britain's answer was **the navy and credit**: sailors from 15,000 to 133,000 [story-revolutions-navy-expansion]; 27 ships of the line at Trafalgar against 33 [story-revolutions-trafalgar-british-fleet] [story-revolutions-trafalgar-fleet]; an income tax that raised just over £6 million in 1799 [story-revolutions-pitt-income-tax]; subsidies of £46 million to 1814 [story-revolutions-britain-subsidies-total], paying for about 450,000 allied soldiers at the peak in 1813 [story-revolutions-britain-subsidy-peak]; and a debt of £679 million [story-revolutions-britain-debt].

The seven coalitions: 1793 [story-revolutions-first-coalition-1793], 1798 [story-revolutions-second-coalition-1798], April 1805 [story-revolutions-third-coalition-1805], October 1806 [story-revolutions-fourth-coalition-1806], April 1809 [story-revolutions-fifth-coalition-1809], the Sixth at Leipzig [story-revolutions-leipzig-coalition], the Seventh at Waterloo. Each lost, re-formed, and added someone. In 1812 Napoleon entered Russia with over 450,000 [story-revolutions-russia-initial-force] (600,000 counting all columns [story-revolutions-russia-deployed-600k]); Russia's army peaked at 904,000 [story-revolutions-russia-army-peak-1812]. In 1815 Napoleon's 74,500 at Waterloo [story-revolutions-french-waterloo-army] met Wellington and 50,000 Prussians [story-revolutions-waterloo-prussians]; he abdicated four days later [story-revolutions-napoleon-abdicated]. The Congress of Vienna, five Great Powers including France [story-revolutions-vienna-five-powers], aimed not at old boundaries but at powers that could balance each other [story-revolutions-vienna-goal]; the Concert of Europe held to 1914 [archetypes-vienna-concert-of-europe].

## What the strategist predicts from it

1. A state that overweights one currency (France: scale, then command) is beaten by the coalition of everyone else; the balance restores itself, as it did in 1814-1815 and again in 1918 and 1945.
2. The side with credit outlasts the side with conscripts: Britain's debt was above 200% of GDP in its own accounts and it still paid for the allies.
3. Legitimacy is an asset that is spent: the empire's plebiscite, the Pope's presence and the Concordat bought recognition only from Austria.
